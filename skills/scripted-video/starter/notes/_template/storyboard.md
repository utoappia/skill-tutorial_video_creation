## Checks before scripting

- [ ] The first 10 seconds show the question and why the viewer should care.
- [ ] Every idea marked `core` in the mind map has a row; nothing appears that is not in the map.
- [ ] Each row needs only ideas from the rows above it.
- [ ] Each new word has its own row, and nothing else happens in that row.
- [ ] There is a pause-and-think row before each answer worth working out. The narrator says a
      quick quiz is coming and reads the question aloud; write the question in "The voice says".
- [ ] Each chapter is under about three minutes and ends on its point. A long video has many
      chapters; a detail of more than a minute has its own.
- [ ] The core rows alone tell the whole video and reach the promise.
- [ ] Each real event has its essentials: the people, the date, the means, the numbers, how it
      ended and its link to the main topic. In a video of ten minutes or more, an event with
      fewer than about ten rows is thin: go back to the research.
- [ ] No detail needs another detail. No core row needs a detail.
- [ ] Nothing the core needs (the promise, a real story's essentials, fairness) was left off this
      page for length. What the core does not need stays in the notes.
- [ ] The last rows repeat the main points in the same words, then say what to do next.
- [ ] The picture changes at least every 20 seconds.
- [ ] At least one real event is told, with its dates (or the brief says "none, because …"). A story of winning says what the winner
      risked, in the core, and has a story of losing beside it.
- [ ] Every guess in the table above that differs from the video has a row that answers it.
- [ ] Most rows have no sound. No row with a new word, a new idea, a quiz or the recap has music.
