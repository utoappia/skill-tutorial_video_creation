# Where a viewer could get lost: {{title}}

Go through the video line by line, in order. For **every** line, write down every way a
first-time viewer could misunderstand it or fall behind, even unlikely ones, then decide what
to do. A short honest list per line beats "fine". Print the lines with
`node tools/dump.mjs {{id}} --text`.

For each line ask:

1. **Words:** is there a word that has not been taught? A word with a second everyday meaning?
2. **Needs:** what must the viewer already know for this to make sense? Where was it taught?
3. **Pointing:** does "this", "it", "here", "that one" point at exactly one thing on screen?
4. **Load:** more than one new idea? A number with no picture? A sum done in the head?
5. **Eyes:** where is the viewer looking when this is said, and is that where the point is?
6. **Speed:** is there time to read what is on screen and to think before the next line?
   A question for the viewer is read aloud, never left only on screen.
7. **Wrong picture:** what false idea could the picture or the comparison plant?
8. **Why care:** does the viewer know why they are being told this?

9. **The guess:** what does the viewer expect to happen next? If the video does something else,
   does it say so aloud?
10. **"So":** does each "so" and "that is why" follow from what was *said*?
11. **Against an earlier line:** does this seem to contradict something said before (a price that
    was "fixed" and now "falls")?
