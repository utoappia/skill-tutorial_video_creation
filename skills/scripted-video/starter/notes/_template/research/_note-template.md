## <A finding, as a heading the mind map can point at>

- One fact per bullet or table row, with its number **and unit**, the date it was true, what
  exactly it measures, and its source with the place in it [1, p. 12].
- Mark how sure: **confirmed** (two independent sources, or one primary source) · **single
  source** · **secondary only** (several accounts, none primary) · **disputed** · **worked
  out** (show the sum) · **search summary only** · **general knowledge, unverified** ·
  **unconfirmed**. The last three are leads, not sources.

## For a real event: one section for each of these

Delete this heading and write the sections. Each question the note cannot answer is a lead.

1. **Background:** the rule, promise or arrangement the event broke; when it began and why.
2. **The people:** each by full name, with their role on that day and who they answered to.
   Include the ones usually left out: the deputy, the one who warned, the one who checked.
3. **The timeline:** a table in date order, in clock time for the decisive days, with a source
   for each row. Mark the times that are disputed.
4. **The means:** how exactly it was done, step by step, with amounts. Exactly which part the
   video's subject played, and which part it did not.
5. **The rules at the time:** what was allowed, what limits applied, who was meant to check.
6. **The warnings:** who saw it coming, when, in what words, and what was done about each.
7. **The numbers:** every figure with unit, date, what it measures and who counted it. The key
   number over time, as a table that could become a chart (one kind of measure only).
8. **What was said:** exact words, each traced to where it was first printed.
9. **The turning point:** where it could have gone the other way, and what each side knew.
10. **Who gained and who lost:** each party, how much, what kind of loss.
11. **The findings:** what inquiries, courts and historians concluded, in their words; who was
    blamed; what could not be established.
12. **What came after:** each person, each organisation, the rules that changed.

## Everyday comparisons

the comparison breaks (every comparison does; a viewer who pushes it too far learns something false).

## For a beginner

Each term and each step of this note in plain words, as you would say it to someone new to the
subject, and where that plain version simplifies. The script's word cards and lines start here.

## Other views and counter-examples

What someone who disagrees would say, in a form they would accept as fair. Cases where the
main claim does not hold. How strong each one is, and its source. For a story of winning: how
it could have failed, and what the winner stood to lose.
