# Leads ledger: {{title}}

A **lead** is anything a source or a note mentions that the notes do not yet explain: a person,
an organisation, a place, a document, a rule, a date, a number, a term, a mechanism given
without its steps, a cause given without its reason, a quotation not yet traced, two sources
that disagree, a question a curious viewer would ask next.

**The main topic, in one sentence:** … (the promise in the brief, and the real stories chosen
to show it).

After writing or extending a note, read it sentence by sentence and add every lead here. **Test
each one before spending a search on it:** if the answer came back, could a sentence with it
help this viewer understand the main topic? A lead met while researching another lead is tested
against the main topic, not against the lead that raised it.

Research a lead that passes **to the depth of a detail and no deeper**: each person's later
life, each document in full and an institution's whole history are another video's research.

A lead ends in one of five ways:

- **researched**: the answer is in the notes (say which note and heading);
- **not found**: you looked and the sources do not say (say what you searched and read);
- **next video**: a neighbouring subject with a story of its own that touches the main topic.
  Write its row in `next-videos.md` with what you already know; research it only as far as the
  core needs;
- **off topic**: it does not bear on the main topic, or lies below the depth of a detail. Say
  why in a few words. Do not research it;
- **open**: on topic, and the rounds ended before it was settled (say what would close it). It
  stays out of the video.

Above that depth, "the core will not say that" does not close an on-topic lead: researching it
is how you learn whether the core needs it.
