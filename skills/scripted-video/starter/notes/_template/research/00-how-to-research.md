## What I learned about researching this field

- **Who knows:** the institutions, datasets, textbooks and people that others quote.
- **Primary sources:** where the numbers and the original documents actually live. For an
  event: the official inquiry, the court's judgment, the institution's own record, newspapers
  of that day, the words of the people who were there.
- **The words of the field:** terms to search with (and to teach, or avoid, in the video).
- **How a beginner is usually taught it:** the order good courses use, and the examples they pick.
- **Traps:** out-of-date numbers, units that differ by country, popular claims that are wrong,
  sources with something to sell.

## Research plan

One line per note to write. Name each file so its content is obvious from the name. Each real
event the video may tell gets a note of its own, researched in depth (the template lists the
twelve things such a note must hold). If other agents can be started, give each event to one.

Research runs in **three rounds**: the outline (these notes); the detail (list every lead in
`leads.md`, test each against the main topic, research those that pass); the double check (read
again, at its source, everything the video will say). Depth on the main topic comes first:
nothing on it is dropped for being "too detailed", and nothing off it is followed for being
interesting.
