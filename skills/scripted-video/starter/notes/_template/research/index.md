**Where the research stopped, and why:** … (the rounds were done · an allowance ran out · a
source would not open). **Open:** … leads, listed in leads.md with what would close each.

After rounds 1 and 2, read every note again from the top, then answer:

- Which person, organisation, document, date, number, term or cause is named and not explained?
- Which figure has not been followed back to whoever counted it? What does each one measure?
- Which claims rest on a single source, or on accounts that copy each other?
- Which quotation has not been traced to where it was first printed?
- Where do two sources disagree?
- Which idea still has no everyday comparison, and which has no real example?
- Is the best case **against** the main claim written down fairly?
- What is commonly said about this that the careful sources do not support?
- What would a curious viewer ask next that the notes cannot answer?

Each answer is a lead. Test it against the main topic before researching it, and research it
no deeper than a detail. "Interesting" is not a reason to follow one that is off topic.
